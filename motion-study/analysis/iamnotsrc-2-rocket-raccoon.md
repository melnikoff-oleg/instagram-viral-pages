## What the video is doing

- **One speed clip, then four "action" clips.** The speed clip opens the video. After that the subject's body is still and the gun provides the motion: flash, recoil, smoke, casings. Light that flickers at 1–2 Hz is an energy source as strong as travel.
- **The still clips are never visually still.** Each has an emitter: muzzle flash, falling embers, flying brass, a city of lights under a moving camera.
- **Only one camera move in the whole video** (the whip pan in 4), and it is a single, fast, horizontal move. Everything else is locked.
- **Backlighting and silhouettes** (moon, light rig, city bokeh) hide detail and sell realism; the raccoon is mostly a dark shape with a lit edge.
- **Realism:** real-looking lens flares from the muzzle, lit smoke, sparks that fall under gravity, defocused city bokeh, film grain, low-light noise. Weak spots: the hands and the gun lose shape under the flash; nobody notices because it is lit for a frame.
- **Hook:** frame 0 is the bike already at speed with streaking lights. Highest-energy clip first.

## Lessons for cars

- Clip 1 is exactly our "straight and fast" shot: side profile, matched tracking, streaking lights, sparks. Low camera height, slightly behind the subject.
- The "emitter" idea translates: exhaust flames, backfire pops, sparks from the undertray, headlights raking through dust, tail lights in rain. If the car must be slower for a beat, an emitter keeps the frame alive.
- Whip pan = one fast horizontal move from a fixed spot. Usable, but only once per reel.
