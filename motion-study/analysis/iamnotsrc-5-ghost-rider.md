## What the video is doing

- **The simplest video of the five, and the most viral.** Two shots, one vector each, one camera behaviour each: side-track, then locked rear view. No camera arcs, no hero close-ups.
- **The emitter is the hero.** The fire does three jobs: it shows speed (trail length), it lights the scene (orange on asphalt), and it keeps the frame alive after the subject leaves (clip 2's last 4 s are just embers dying on a road).
- **Screen direction is consistent**: left→right, then away. Never reverses.
- **Clip 2 is the perfect "car drives away" shot.** Locked camera, straight road, subject shrinks, trail stays. It is the easiest shot to generate and the hardest to get wrong.
- **Realism:** twilight gradient, real road markings, embers that land and stay, heat haze over the fire, mountains with atmospheric perspective, grain. The flames are stylised but behave: they trail, they spread on the ground, they die.
- **Hook:** frame 0 is bike + flames + sunset, fully readable, already moving.

## Lessons for cars

- Our side-profile straight shot = clip 1. Keep the camera a touch slower than the car so the car gains on the frame; that tiny drift in frame position is what makes matched tracking feel alive instead of pinned.
- Our "drive away" ending = clip 2, with dust, snow or rain spray as the trail and tail lights as the emitter. Let the trail outlive the car.
- Fewer, longer, simpler clips beat more, shorter, busier ones. Two clips carried 4 million plays.
