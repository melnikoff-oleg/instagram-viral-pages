## What the video is doing

- **Two clips only, 7 s and 8 s.** The longest clips of the five reels, and the best-performing of the four long reels. Length is fine when the frame is alive the whole time.
- **Clip 1 is the "drift" archetype.** The subject does not travel across the frame much; it rotates, carves and sheds spray. The camera rolls with him. This is the one place in the five reels where the move is not a straight line, and it still reads because it is one continuous carving action, backlit, with spray.
- **Clip 2 is the "straight line with a trail".** A trail (exhaust, dust, light) is the cheapest way to make a straight line look fast and to tell the viewer where the subject has been.
- **Backlight sells everything.** A silhouette against a hot source hides surface detail, so the generator has little to get wrong, and the haze, spray and bloom read as physics.
- **Realism:** sun bloom, haze, particles in the light, horizon curvature, the trail dissipating. Weak spot: the board and body lose shape in the brightest frames.
- **Hook:** frame 0 is the subject mid-carve, already backlit and moving.

## Lessons for cars

- The drift shot is clip 1: car rotating around its own axis, smoke/sand/snow shed from the rear tyres, camera close and low, horizon allowed to roll slightly (only here, and only because the move is one continuous arc).
- Clip 2 maps to "car on a straight road with a dust/snow/rain plume behind it, matched tracking, landmark sliding past".
- Backlight the car: low sun or a single hot light behind it, headlights toward the camera. Detail in shadow is detail the model cannot ruin.
